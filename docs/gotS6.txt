Episode 1: The Red Woman
In the North, at Winterfell, Ramsay Bolton stands over the body of Myranda, the woman who had helped torment Sansa. He speaks to Maester Wolkan about how she never feared him the way others did. When asked about burial or a pyre, Ramsay coldly orders her body fed to his hounds. Roose Bolton confronts his son about the recent victory over Stannis Baratheon’s army. He points out that Sansa’s escape has weakened their position; a Lannister force marching north could crush them. Ramsay insists his best men and hounds will recapture her. Roose warns that if Ramsay fails, the unborn child of his new wife Walda will become the true heir instead.

Meanwhile, Sansa Stark and Theon Greyjoy have survived their desperate jump from Winterfell’s walls. They flee through the snowy Wolfswood as Bolton men and hounds pursue them. Theon tries to throw off the scent by crossing a freezing river, then holds Sansa close for warmth behind a fallen tree. The dogs close in. Theon attempts to lead them away, telling Sansa to head north to Castle Black and Jon Snow. Bolton soldiers catch them. Theon lies that Sansa died in the fall, but the hounds find her. The leader mocks Theon about the horrors Ramsay will inflict. Just then Brienne of Tarth and Podrick Payne arrive. Brienne cuts down most of the men with skilled, brutal efficiency. Podrick fights one using the techniques Brienne taught him and kills him. Another knocks Podrick down, but Theon seizes a sword and finishes the attacker. Sansa, seeing Brienne’s loyalty and strength, formally accepts her into service. They prepare to continue north.

In Braavos, blind Arya Stark begs on the streets. The Waif approaches with fighting sticks and forces one into Arya’s hands. Arya protests she cannot fight while blind. The Waif replies that is not her problem and beats Arya repeatedly. Arya flails weakly in defense. The Waif stops and says they will meet again the next day, leaving Arya bruised and determined.

In Meereen, Tyrion Lannister and Varys walk the streets disguised as merchants. They encounter a starving woman with a baby. Tyrion offers a coin for the child to eat, but his poor High Valyrian makes her think he wants to buy the baby to eat it. Varys quickly explains. The woman accepts. As they continue, Tyrion stresses the need to find the leader of the Sons of the Harpy to restore peace. Varys says his little birds are already gathering information. Suddenly a screaming crowd flees a massive cloud of smoke. The ships in the harbor are all ablaze. Tyrion dryly notes they will not be sailing to Westeros anytime soon.

In the Dothraki Sea, Daario Naharis and Jorah Mormont track Daenerys. They find the charred remains of a ram killed by Drogon’s fire and many hoof prints. Jorah spots the ring Daenerys deliberately dropped as a trail. Meanwhile Daenerys herself is bound and brought before Khal Moro as a prize. Moro intends to rape her, but she declares she is the widow of Khal Drogo. Moro unbinds her and promises safety. She offers a thousand horses for safe return to Meereen. Moro’s wives explain that as a khal’s widow she must go to Vaes Dothrak and join the dosh khaleen, living out her days among the other widows in the temple. She has no choice.

In Dorne’s Water Gardens, Ellaria Sand helps Prince Doran Martell into his wheelchair. They speak of Oberyn; Doran envied his brother’s adventurous life while Ellaria notes Doran would have been a poor adventurer and Oberyn a poor ruler. Maester Caleotte brings a message that Myrcella is dead. Before Doran can react, Tyene Sand stabs Areo Hotah in the back, killing him. Ellaria stabs Doran in the chest. His guards do nothing; they have sided with her. As she twists the knife she rages that he stayed passive while his sister Elia and brother Oberyn were murdered. The people of Dorne are with her now. Dying, Doran begs her to spare his son Trystane. She scoffs. Doran dies in a pool of blood.

In King’s Landing, Cersei sits alone until a servant announces a ship from Dorne. She runs joyfully to the harbor, believing Myrcella has returned, only to see Jaime standing grimly beside a shrouded body. She realizes her daughter is dead and collapses in grief. Later in the Red Keep she asks Jaime when he first saw a dead body; he answers it was their mother’s. She describes her own horror at their mother’s corpse and says she does not want to look upon Myrcella’s. She reveals Maggy the Frog’s prophecy to him, but Jaime dismisses it as nonsense.

Margaery remains imprisoned by the Faith. The High Sparrow visits and asks if she is ready to confess. She admits no one is without sin. He says she is on the right path but still has far to go. Meanwhile Obara and Nymeria Sand board Trystane’s ship. They announce they will kill him. Nymeria offers him the choice of opponent; he picks her. As he faces Nymeria, Obara drives a spear through the back of his head, killing him.

At the Wall, Jon Snow’s body still lies where the mutineers left it. Ghost howls and scratches at the door. Davos Seaworth investigates and finds the body just as Eddison Tollett and other loyal brothers arrive. They carry Jon inside. Edd closes Jon’s eyes and curses Alliser Thorne. Davos asks whom Edd trusts; Edd answers only those in the room. Davos has him bring Ghost inside for protection. Melisandre arrives, saying she saw Jon fighting at Winterfell in the flames. The others doubt a dead man can fight. She withdraws.

Thorne, Othell Yarwyck, and Bowen Marsh address the Night’s Watch brothers, justifying the murder of Jon for bringing wildlings through the Wall. Most brothers side with them. Inside the locked room with Jon’s body, Davos notes Thorne will move soon. Edd wants revenge but Davos points out they are badly outnumbered. He reminds them of the wildlings nearby who owe Jon their lives. Edd slips out to fetch them while the others bar the door.

Thorne approaches with armed men and offers Davos and the loyalists full amnesty, a horse, and safe passage south, even taking Melisandre. Davos mocks the offer by requesting mutton so he will not starve. Thorne says food will be provided if they surrender by sundown.

Alone in her chambers, Melisandre looks defeated. She undresses before a mirror, removes her glowing ruby necklace, and her youthful glamour fades. She becomes a frail, ancient woman with sagging skin, thin white hair, and a bent body. Despondent, she climbs into bed naked and covers herself.

Episode 2: Home
Beyond the Wall, Bran Stark continues training with the Three-Eyed Raven (Brynden Rivers). In a vision he watches young Ned and Benjen Stark sparring in Winterfell’s yard. He sees young Lyanna Stark, Old Nan, and Ser Rodrik Cassel. He also sees a young stableboy who is Hodor—then called Wylis—who speaks normally and is not simple-minded. Bran wants to stay longer but the Raven pulls him out, warning that lingering in visions is dangerous. Outside, Meera Reed still grieves her brother Jojen. Leaf the Child of the Forest tells her Bran will need her when he eventually leaves the cave.

In King’s Landing a drunken man boasts in a tavern about mocking Cersei during her walk of atonement. Outside he is confronted by Ser Gregor Clegane (the Mountain), who smashes his head against the wall, killing him, then returns to Cersei’s side.

Myrcella’s funeral is held in the Great Sept of Baelor, but Cersei is confined to the Red Keep by Tommen’s order for fear the Faith Militant will re-arrest her. Jaime and Tommen speak of Myrcella and Trystane. Tommen blames Cersei somewhat for Trystane’s death but is not angry. Jaime urges him to visit and apologize to his mother. The High Sparrow enters. After Tommen leaves, Jaime threatens the High Sparrow, asking why Cersei is punished for sins while the Sparrow is not punished for Jaime’s own past crime of killing the Mad King he swore to protect. Jaime nearly kills him, but the High Sparrow shows no fear. He explains that individual Sparrows mean nothing; together they can topple empires.

Tommen visits Cersei and apologizes for not protecting her more strongly. He says he should have torn the sept down with the High Sparrow inside. He needs her help to be a strong king. Cersei, uncharacteristically quiet, embraces him.

In Meereen, Missandei and Grey Worm tell Tyrion that Rhaegal and Viserion have stopped eating since Daenerys left. Tyrion says dragons hate captivity and may eat again if freed. He believes they remember friends. Tyrion and Varys go below the pyramid. Tyrion advances alone into the darkness. One dragon emerges; Tyrion holds its gaze. The second appears. He sets down his torch, speaks gently of how as a child he wanted a dragon for his nameday and how his father told him they were extinct, making him cry. He reaches the first collar and releases the pin, then frees the second. The dragons retreat. Tyrion hurries back and tells Varys to punch him if he ever suggests anything like that again.

In Braavos, the Waif again approaches blind Arya and asks her name. Arya says “no one.” The Waif hits her, saying neither of them believes it. Arya tries to fight back. Suddenly the Waif is replaced by Jaqen H’ghar. He promises that if she says her name she will sleep under a roof, eat, and regain her sight. Each time Arya insists she has no name. Satisfied, Jaqen leads her back; she is a beggar no more.

At Winterfell, Ramsay, Roose, and Harald Karstark discuss Sansa and Jon. Ramsay wants to storm Castle Black. Roose warns that attacking the Night’s Watch would turn the entire North against them and that Ramsay must act more wisely or be discarded. Maester Wolkan announces that Walda has given birth to a boy. Roose tells Ramsay he will always be treated as firstborn. Ramsay thanks him, then stabs his father to death in front of the maester. As the new Lord Bolton he orders Wolkan to announce that Roose was poisoned by enemies. He personally feeds Walda and the newborn to his hounds.

In the woods Brienne tells Sansa about meeting Arya. Sansa is reluctant to speak of her torment under Ramsay but admits she should have accepted Brienne’s help sooner. Theon, crushed by guilt over betraying the Starks, decides to leave and go home to the Iron Islands rather than continue to Castle Black. He tells Sansa that Brienne and Podrick will protect her better. They embrace; he takes a horse and departs.

On the Iron Islands, at Pyke on a stormy night, Yara Greyjoy reads a letter to King Balon: the Glovers have retaken Deepwood Motte and killed the Ironborn garrison. Their northern invasion has failed. Balon wants to keep fighting. Yara argues the mainland strongholds are worthless to Ironborn so far from the sea, and that her rescue attempt for Theon was not a waste. She reminds him of the last Greyjoy Rebellion that cost two of her brothers. Balon says when she rules she can be content; until then she obeys or he will make another heir.

Balon crosses a rope bridge between towers. A man blocks his way—his younger brother Euron Greyjoy, long thought lost. Euron mocks the Drowned God and claims he is the god. He admits cutting out his crew’s tongues for silence and that he once had to be tied to the mast in a storm. He says Balon has ruled too long. Balon tries to stab him; Euron throws him off the bridge to his death.

At Balon’s funeral Yara swears to Aeron Greyjoy (another brother and priest) that she will feed the killer to sharks alive. Aeron says the kingsmoot will choose the next ruler, not her father’s wish.

At Castle Black night has fallen. The loyalists still hold the room with Jon’s body. Thorne’s men prepare to batter the door. Edd returns with Tormund and a host of wildlings, including the giant Wun Wun. Thorne orders the Watch to fight; only one foolish brother charges and is cut down. An archer shoots Wun Wun, who seizes him, smashes him against stone, and throws the body at Thorne’s feet. The rest surrender. Thorne is seized; Olly is disarmed by Tormund. No wildlings die.

Later Melisandre, Davos, and the loyalists stand over Jon’s body. Melisandre performs a ritual, cutting his hair, washing him, and chanting over him in the name of the Lord of Light. Nothing happens. She leaves disappointed. Davos and Edd leave too. Ghost, who had been under the table, stirs. Jon gasps and awakens.

Episode 3: Oathbreaker
At the Wall Jon Snow sits up, gasping, staring at his stab wounds in horror. Davos helps him. Jon remembers being stabbed by Olly and Thorne for bringing the wildlings south. Melisandre enters, amazed the resurrection worked. She asks what he saw after death. Jon says nothing. Disappointed yet hopeful, she declares the Lord of Light brought him back for a reason; Stannis was not the Prince That Was Promised, but someone must be. Davos asks her to leave so Jon can adjust.

Davos notes the situation is completely mad. Jon laments he tried to do right and was murdered for it; why was he brought back? Davos says they may never know, but the point is to keep living and fighting. Jon feels he failed. Davos replies, “Good. Now go fail again.”

Jon dons his armor and Lord Commander’s cloak and walks into the courtyard. Free Folk and brothers gather. He greets a stunned Tormund, who hugs him (hurting the wounds). He embraces Edd. Jon jokes he is still himself, not a wight, so hold off burning the body.

Later Jon oversees the hanging of the mutineers: Bowen Marsh, Othell Yarwyck, Alliser Thorne, and Olly. Marsh says Jon should not be alive. Jon replies neither should killing him have been. Yarwyck asks Jon to write his mother that he died fighting wildlings honorably. Thorne says he chose the Watch over his commander; Jon’s wildling alliance was too dangerous. Jon respects that Thorne followed his convictions. Olly glares in silence. Jon cuts the rope. All four die. Jon removes his cloak, hands it to Edd, and declares his watch has ended. He walks away as the brothers stare in shock.

On the Narrow Sea Samwell Tarly, Gilly, and little Sam sail toward Oldtown. Sam is seasick. Gilly is excited; the captain called Oldtown the most beautiful city. Sam realizes he cannot take Gilly into the all-male Citadel, so he decides to stop first at his family home of Horn Hill. His mother and sister are kind even if his father Randyll is not. Gilly agrees to follow his lead for their safety.

Beyond the Wall Bran has another vision: the Tower of Joy in Dorne at the end of Robert’s Rebellion. Young Ned Stark and a small party including Howland Reed (Meera’s father) confront the last two Targaryen Kingsguard—Ser Arthur Dayne (Sword of the Morning) and Lord Commander Gerold Hightower. Ned asks why they were not at the Trident. Dayne says Rhaegar ordered them to guard the tower. They draw swords. “And now it begins,” Dayne says. “No, now it ends,” Ned replies. Dayne fights with two swords and cuts down several northmen. Hightower falls to Ned. Dayne duels Ned and gains the upper hand, kicking away Ned’s sword—until Howland stabs him from behind through the neck. Ned finishes him, uncomfortable with the dishonorable win. Bran is shocked; the stories never mentioned this. Ned hears a woman’s scream from the tower and runs toward it. Bran tries to follow, calling out. The Raven pulls him back, saying he stayed too long. The past cannot be changed. The Raven has waited a thousand years for Bran; Bran must learn everything before leaving, but he will not share the Raven’s fate.

In King’s Landing Qyburn gathers Varys’s former little birds (child spies) with candied plums from Dorne. Cersei, Jaime, and the Mountain enter. Cersei wants information from Dorne, Highgarden, the North—anywhere enemies plot against them.

At the Small Council, Grand Maester Pycelle complains about Qyburn. Kevan Lannister (Hand of the King), Pycelle, and Mace Tyrell sit with Olenna Tyrell lobbying for Margaery and Loras’s release. Cersei, Jaime, and Gregor barge in. Cersei claims her right as queen; Olenna corrects that the true queen is Margaery, married to the king. Jaime claims his right as Lord Commander of the Kingsguard. Pycelle notes Gerold Hightower once sat on the council but dismisses it as the Mad King’s eccentricity. Cersei accuses Kevan of not caring about Myrcella. Jaime urges action against Ellaria and the Sand Snakes who seized Dorne. Kevan and the others leave in displeasure.

Tommen angrily visits the High Sparrow seeking permission for Cersei to visit Myrcella’s resting place. The Sparrow refuses until her other sins are cleared. Tommen criticizes the treatment after her atonement. The Sparrow admires Cersei’s love for her son as divine Motherly love and says the Seven work even through those who do not acknowledge them. Tommen leaves less angry, more uncertain.

In Meereen Varys interrogates Vala, a prostitute who seduced and murdered Second Sons and Unsullied for the Sons of the Harpy. She is defiant, claiming patriotism for the old ways. Varys offers her and her son safe passage to Pentos with silver if she names the funders; otherwise execution and her son orphaned. She agrees. The Wise Masters of Yunkai, Good Masters of Astapor, and Volantis have been funding the Harpies. Grey Worm wants to attack the cities; Missandei warns that would leave Meereen undefended. Tyrion has Varys send a message via the little birds.

In Vaes Dothrak Daenerys enters the temple of the dosh khaleen. (The full arrival and initial interactions continue her captivity among the widows.)

Episode 4: Book of the Stranger
In King’s Landing beneath the Great Sept, Septa Unella leads Margaery to the High Sparrow. He asks what she would do if freed. She wants her family and husband Tommen. He lectures that attachment leads to sin, recounting his own past as a womanizing, drunken cobbler who converted after an orgy. Margaery impresses him by knowing the Book of the Stranger (which Unella had read at her). He allows her to visit Loras.

Loras lies dejected in his cell, broken. Margaery urges him to stay strong; he is the future of House Tyrell. He only wants the torment to end and begs her help. She says the Sparrow wants them to break each other.

In the Red Keep Cersei finds Pycelle advising Tommen to accommodate the Faith. She dismisses Pycelle and speaks alone with her son. Tommen wants to avoid endangering Margaery further. Cersei reminds him of her own humiliation and that the Sparrow respects no kings or queens. Tommen reveals a secret the Sparrow shared.

Armed with it, Cersei interrupts a Small Council meeting of Kevan, Olenna, and Jaime. They are hostile at first. She reveals the High Sparrow plans a walk of atonement for Margaery. Olenna is horrified. Jaime proposes the Tyrell army march into the city, surround the Sept, and force the release of Margaery and Loras without bloodshed (or with Sparrow blood). Tommen forbade only the Lannister army from acting, so Kevan’s forces can simply stand down. Kevan hesitates until Cersei reminds him the Sparrows took his son Lancel. Olenna agrees blood will be shed either way; better the Sparrows’.

At Castle Black Edd watches Jon pack. He scolds Jon for leaving after his oath. Jon replies he already gave his life and cannot trust the brothers. Edd reminds him of Hardhome and the coming enemy. Horns sound. Sansa, Brienne, and Podrick ride in. Jon and Sansa embrace for the first time since childhood. That night they reminisce. Sansa wants to retake Winterfell; Jon says he is done fighting after hanging Olly.

Davos asks if Melisandre will stay. She will do as the Prince That Was Promised commands. Davos presses about Stannis and Shireen; she is reluctant. Brienne enters, recognizes them as Stannis’s people, and warns Melisandre she neither forgets nor forgives Renly’s murder. She reveals she executed Stannis after he admitted using bloodmagic.

Jon receives Ramsay’s “Bastard Letter”: Ramsay has Rickon. If Sansa is not returned he will slaughter every wildling at Castle Black, rape Sansa, and feed Jon and Rickon to his dogs. Tormund has only about 2,000 fighters against Ramsay’s estimated 5,000. Sansa insists the northern houses will unite behind Jon as Ned Stark’s son. Jon agrees to fight.

At Runestone young Robin Arryn practices (badly) at archery under Yohn Royce. Petyr Baelish arrives with a falcon gift. Royce confronts him: Baelish said he was taking Sansa to the Fingers, yet she married Ramsay. Baelish claims Bolton men attacked them and accuses Royce of leaking her location. Robin, as Lord of the Vale, is asked to judge. Robin considers the Moon Door. Royce swears loyalty; Baelish recommends mercy. Royce realizes Littlefinger controls Robin. Baelish and Robin decide to muster the Vale knights and march north to aid Sansa.

At Winterfell Osha is brought before Ramsay. He values Rickon but is unsure about her. She denounces the Starks as forced masters and tries to seduce him, climbing onto his lap while reaching for his knife. Ramsay reveals Theon told him everything, including how she once seduced Theon to free Bran and Rickon. She lunges; he slits her throat and calmly resumes peeling an apple as she dies.

On Pyke Theon returns home to learn Balon is dead. Yara is angry he refused her earlier rescue. He says Ramsay broke him into a thousand pieces (she notes they received one piece in a box). She thinks he wants the crown; he insists he wants her to rule and will support her.

In Meereen Tyrion, Grey Worm, Missandei, and Varys meet envoys from the masters of Astapor, Yunkai, and Volantis (including Yezzan zo Qaggaz, whom Tyrion once served as a slave). Grey Worm wants force. Tyrion claims empathy from one day as a slave; Missandei says that is not true slavery. They negotiate terms for peace and the end of funding the Harpies. (Tyrion’s deal-making continues, securing a fragile truce while Daenerys is still absent.)

Episode 5: The Door
At the Wall Sansa receives a message from Littlefinger requesting a meeting in Mole’s Town. With Brienne she confronts him furiously about knowing what Ramsay was. He claims ignorance and offers the knights of the Vale waiting at Moat Cailin. She describes her wedding night torment; he looks horrified and apologizes, offering protection. She doubts him and threatens Brienne will kill him. He will do whatever she asks. She orders him never to return. As he leaves he informs her that Brynden “Blackfish” Tully has retaken Riverrun and she should seek his help. She says she already has Jon’s wildling army. “Half-brother,” he corrects.

A war council at Castle Black: Sansa, Brienne, Podrick, Jon, Davos, Melisandre, Tormund, Edd. The Karstarks and Umbers have declared for Ramsay. They must summon the remaining northern houses—Mormont, Glover, Cerwyn, Mazin. Sansa says “the North remembers” and will fight for the Stark name. She reveals the Blackfish holds Riverrun (claiming she learned it from Ramsay’s ravens to hide Littlefinger’s involvement). She sends Brienne and Podrick to secure Tully help while the rest build an army. Before leaving she gives Jon a new cloak bearing the Stark direwolf, like their father’s.

In Braavos the Waif drills Arya, mocking her high birth. Jaqen explains the Faceless Men began as Valyrian slaves who founded Braavos and the House of Black and White. He gives Arya a vial of poison; her target is the actress Lady Crane. Arya watches a play, The Bloody Hand, a crude retelling of recent Westerosi wars. She enjoys the spectacle until her father Ned is portrayed as a buffoon and the actress playing Sansa bares her breasts. Afterward she observes Lady Crane (playing Cersei), who seems decent. Arya plans to poison Crane’s private rum, suspecting a jealous younger actress Bianca hired the kill. Jaqen cuts her off: a servant does not question.

In Vaes Dothrak Daenerys struggles with what to do about Jorah, who has returned twice after banishment and saved her life twice. He confesses his love but also shows his spreading greyscale. This time he must leave for good and will end his life before the disease takes him fully. She tearfully orders him to find a cure and return when she conquers Westeros. He departs. Daenerys and Daario lead the Dothraki horde toward Meereen.

In Meereen, with a fragile peace holding, Tyrion wants the people to believe everything is done with Daenerys’s blessing so the masters cannot use his and Varys’s foreign status against them. He invites Kinvara, High Priestess of the Red Temple of Volantis. She readily agrees to spread word that Daenerys is the Prince That Was Promised. Varys is skeptical, recalling Melisandre’s failed declaration for Stannis. Kinvara says humans err even when the Lord of Light has a plan. She unnerves Varys by knowing exactly how a second-rate sorcerer castrated him and offering to repeat the words he heard in the flames. She promises to send suitable preachers.

On the Iron Islands the kingsmoot is held on Old Wyk. Yara claims the Salt Throne first, arguing they must do more than raid—they must teach the mainland a lesson with real force. A man challenges her for being a woman and notes Theon’s return. Theon endorses Yara as a true warrior and reaver. Many shout for her. Euron arrives, mocks Theon’s emasculation and Yara’s sex, admits killing Balon because he was leading them to defeat, and regrets not doing it sooner. The crowd cheers. Theon notes Euron was away while Yara commanded ships. Yara proposes a massive fleet to strike Westeros. Euron agrees to expand the fleet and reveals his plan: marry Daenerys Targaryen and bring her army and dragons to Westeros. The Ironborn acclaim him king. Aeron drowns and revives him in the traditional rite. Yara, Theon, and their loyalists flee during the ceremony with the best ships of the Iron Fleet. Euron, upon learning, orders new ships built to pursue and kill them.

Beyond the Wall the Three-Eyed Raven shows Bran a vision of the creation of the first White Walker: Leaf and the Children of the Forest, desperate in their war with the First Men, drive a dragonglass dagger into a captive man’s chest, transforming him into the Night King. Bran confronts Leaf; she says they were losing and had no choice. Later, alone, Bran wargs into the weirwood without permission and walks among the Army of the Dead. The Night King and White Walkers see him. The Night King grabs Bran’s arm, marking him. Bran wakes screaming. The Raven says the Night King can now find him and bypass the cave’s protective magic. They must leave immediately; Bran must become the Three-Eyed Raven. Bran asks if he is ready. “No,” the Raven says, and Bran’s eyes whiten as another vision begins.

Outside, the air turns freezing. The Night King and White Walkers approach. The Children fire magical projectiles and light a fire barrier, but the Walkers extinguish a path and the wights swarm over and through the weirwood roots. Meera kills one Walker with dragonglass and fights beside the remaining Children. Summer the direwolf dies attacking wights. Leaf sacrifices herself in an explosion. Meera screams for Bran to warg into Hodor. In the vision Bran is at Winterfell watching young Ned say goodbye to his father Rickard before going to the Vale as a ward. Hearing Meera, and urged by the Raven, Bran wargs simultaneously into present-day Hodor and young Wylis. Hodor loads Bran onto a sled and hauls him toward the exit tunnel with Meera and Leaf. The Night King kills the Three-Eyed Raven; his form in the vision disintegrates. At the door Meera yells “Hold the door!” Wylis in the past seizes and begins repeating the words; they slur and shorten until he can only say “Hodor.” Present Hodor holds the door against the wights as Meera and Bran escape, sacrificing himself as the undead pour through and tear him apart.

Episode 6: Blood of My Blood
Beyond the Wall Meera pulls Bran’s sled through the snow while he remains locked in cascading visions: Aerys screaming “Burn them all!”, the Red Wedding, his family, Hardhome wights, wildfire, dragons, Jaime and the Iron Throne. Wights close in. Meera collapses, draws her sword, and apologizes for failing him. A mysterious rider appears, cutting down wights with a sickle and flaming flail. He urges them onto his horse because the dead do not rest. Later, safe for the moment, the rider reveals he was sent by the Three-Eyed Raven who “lives again.” Bran wakes. The rider is Benjen Stark, Bran’s uncle—disfigured. A White Walker stabbed him and left him for dead, but the Children saved him and halted the transformation into a full wight. He tells Bran he must now be the Three-Eyed Raven and wait for the White Walkers when they come for the realms of men.

At Horn Hill Sam, Gilly, and little Sam arrive. Sam warns they must claim the baby is his son and hide that Gilly is a wildling; his father hates them. Lady Melessa and sister Talla greet them warmly, delighted at the “grandchild.” Talla offers Gilly a dress and room. At dinner Lord Randyll and brother Dickon join. Sam announces he will train as a maester then return to the Wall. Randyll mocks Sam’s weight and false claims of hunting and fighting skill (which Sam admits belong to Gilly and Jon). Gilly defends Sam by listing his real heroics, accidentally revealing she is from beyond the Wall. Randyll’s prejudice erupts. Melessa, furious, says Randyll is the one dishonoring the house and storms out with the women. Randyll allows Gilly and the baby to stay (she will work in the kitchens; the boy raised as a bastard) but Sam must leave forever at first light. Sam later apologizes to Gilly for not standing up, then returns, saying they stay together. They leave immediately; Sam takes the family Valyrian steel sword Heartsbane.

In King’s Landing Tommen speaks with the High Sparrow about Margaery’s planned walk of atonement. The Sparrow lets him see her. Tommen finds Margaery speaking positively of the Sparrow and contritely about her past vanity. He begins agreeing with her.

Mace Tyrell leads a Tyrell army through the streets to Jaime. Together they reach the Great Sept where the High Sparrow, Unella, Faith Militant, and crowd prepare for the walk. Jaime demands the release of Margaery and Loras. The Sparrow refuses, saying every Sparrow yearns to die for the gods. Then he announces there will be no walk: Margaery has already atoned by bringing another into the Faith. The doors open and Tommen emerges, escorted, wearing a seven-pointed star on his Kingsguard’s breastplates. He joins the Sparrow and Margaery, declaring the Crown and Faith are the two pillars of the realm and will bring glory together. The crowd cheers. Olenna realizes the Sparrow has beaten them. Jaime seethes.

Later Tommen strips Jaime of his Kingsguard position because an attack on the Faith is an attack on the Crown, but allows him to continue serving elsewhere. Jaime complains to Cersei; he wants Bronn to gather killers and storm the Sept. Cersei says he must go to Riverrun instead and take the castle cleanly. She does not need him for her trial—it will be trial by combat and she has the Mountain. They will still defeat their enemies. They kiss.

In Braavos Arya watches the play again. The Cersei monologue has been changed per her earlier suggestion (vengeance instead of pure grief). She is moved by Lady Crane’s performance and notices Bianca mouthing the lines. Backstage she poisons Crane’s rum but hesitates. Crane recognizes her from the audience. They talk; Arya feels kinship. She suggests the original speech was wrong for the real Cersei. Crane is impressed and asks if Arya likes pretending to be someone else. Unsettled, Arya leaves. Crane argues with the company leader about the script. Just as she raises the poisoned rum Arya returns, knocks it away, points at Bianca, and warns her. The Waif witnesses and reports to Jaqen, requesting permission to kill Arya. Jaqen reluctantly agrees—she is not to suffer. Arya retrieves Needle, hides in a dark room, blows out the candle, and waits.

At the Twins Walder Frey learns Riverrun is lost to the Blackfish. He blames his sons for letting the Blackfish escape the Red Wedding. Despite numbers, Houses Mallister and Blackwood have risen and the Brotherhood Without Banners raids their lines. Walder demands the castle back and brings out his prisoner Edmure Tully as leverage, joking that Edmure is going home.

In the Dothraki Sea Daenerys and Daario continue with the khalasar toward Meereen (further travel and planning occur).

Episode 7: The Broken Man
In the Riverlands Sandor Clegane (the Hound), revealed to have survived his fight with Brienne, lives among a peaceful congregation led by Brother Ray, a septon who found him near death. Ray speaks of justice; Sandor says if there were justice he would already be punished. Ray replies perhaps he has been. Brotherhood Without Banners men arrive demanding tribute; finding nothing of value they leave. Sandor warns they will return and that Ray should arm himself. Ray says violence is a disease not cured by spreading it. Sandor answers it is not cured by dying either. He returns from chopping wood to find the villagers slaughtered and Ray hanged. Enraged, he takes an axe and leaves for revenge.

Jaime and Bronn arrive with the Lannister army at Riverrun. The Freys threaten to hang Edmure if the Blackfish does not surrender. The Blackfish calls their bluff. Disgusted by Frey incompetence, Jaime takes command, parleys with the Blackfish, and offers to spare the garrison if they yield. The Blackfish refuses; he has two years of food and will make the Lannisters pay dearly in blood.

In King’s Landing Margaery studies the Seven-Pointed Star with the High Sparrow. They discuss the Mother’s mercy. She admits she once only pitied the poor while pretending to love them. He asks why she has not joined Tommen in the marriage bed; she says former desires are gone. He reminds her of her duty to produce an heir and makes a veiled threat against Olenna as an unrepentant sinner.

Margaery meets Olenna under Unella’s watch. Olenna insults the septa; Margaery defends her. Alone (as much as possible) Olenna tries to pull Margaery away and urges her to leave for Highgarden. Margaery refuses, insisting her place is with Tommen, but secretly slips Olenna a paper with a drawn rose—the Tyrell sigil—signaling she is only pretending loyalty to the Faith and that Olenna should leave for safety. Olenna understands and plays along.

Cersei confronts Olenna about leaving, begging her to stay for Margaery and Loras’s sake. Olenna blames Cersei for arming the Faith Militant in the first place. Cersei admits the mistake but insists their alliance is more vital than ever. Olenna says Cersei has no power left and that Cersei’s defeat is her only consolation; she will leave at once.

In the North Jon, Sansa, and Davos recruit. With Tormund and Wun Wun they secure the wildlings, who still owe Jon for Hardhome. Davos shows Jon and Sansa Lyanna Mormont’s earlier rejection letter to Stannis. At Bear Island the ten-year-old Lady Lyanna Mormont is blunt and unimpressed. She initially refuses, saying House Stark is dead, Jon is a bastard, Sansa has married enemies, and she needs her men to defend her island. Davos intervenes, speaking of the White Walkers’ threat to all living. She agrees to send all 62 men she can spare, each fighting like ten.

At Deepwood Motte Robett Glover gives a cold reception. Robb Stark failed to protect them from the Ironborn; he will not risk more for the remaining Starks. They leave with only minor houses (Mormont, Hornwood, Mazin)—about 405 extra men. Jon wants to attack Winterfell soon before Ramsay gathers more and winter deepens. Sansa wants more houses (Cerwyn). When Jon refuses she secretly writes to Littlefinger accepting his earlier offer of Vale troops.

In Volantis Theon and Yara hide with the Iron Fleet. In a brothel Yara carouses while Theon sits despondent. She pushes him to drink, reclaim himself, and help her take revenge on Euron and retake the Iron Islands. “Fuck justice,” she says; they will get revenge. He drinks and steadies. She reveals the plan: sail to Meereen and ally with Daenerys before Euron can. She then leaves with a prostitute.

In Braavos Arya walks openly, arranges passage back to Westeros with traders for a large sum, then stands on a bridge gazing at the Titan. An old woman approaches—the Waif in disguise—and repeatedly stabs Arya in the gut, twisting the knife. Arya leaps into the canal and stumbles through the streets, bleeding and terrified.

Episode 8: No One
In Braavos the acting troupe performs the revised Bloody Hand with Lady Crane’s vengeful Cersei speech. Afterward Crane finds injured Arya and tends her wounds. They talk of the future; Crane invites Arya to join the company bound for Pentos. Arya warns of coming danger. Crane gives her milk of the poppy so she can sleep and heal.

The next morning Crane reaches for medicine on a high shelf. Arya wakes to a crash and finds Crane murdered—stabbed by the Waif. The Waif rebukes Arya for not completing the original kill (so Crane would have died cleanly) and now comes for her. Arya flees through streets, under a wagon, into a bathhouse, down stairs overturning vegetable baskets. Her wound reopens. She leaves a bloody trail into a darkened room, hides Needle under a blanket, and waits. The Waif enters, closes the door, and offers Arya the choice of dying on her knees or her feet. Arya stands, draws Needle, slices the candle, and plunges the room into blackness.

Later Jaqen enters the Hall of Faces, follows a blood trail, and finds the Waif’s face (eyes gouged) placed on a shelf. Arya confronts him. He admits sending the Waif. He says she has become No One and passed the test. Arya corrects him: she is Arya Stark of Winterfell and she is going home. Jaqen nods with the faintest smile. Arya leaves.

In Meereen red priestesses preach that Daenerys was sent by the Lord of Light to end slavery. Tyrion and Varys walk the market; Varys is leaving on a secret mission to find Daenerys friends in Westeros. He parts before the docks so the famous dwarf is not seen with him. Tyrion corrects: “the most famous dwarf in the world.”

Later Tyrion drinks with Grey Worm and Missandei to celebrate the city’s recovery. They reluctantly join in honor of their queen. Grey Worm dislikes the taste. Tyrion dreams of a vineyard after conquest. They attempt jokes; Missandei tells one about translators; Grey Worm claims it is the worst he has heard, then smiles—he was joking. Suddenly bombardment begins. Slaver ships from Astapor, Yunkai, and Volantis attack with trebuchets. Diplomacy has failed. Grey Worm takes command, preferring to defend the pyramid. Then they hear movement on the roofs: Daenerys has returned on Drogon.

In King’s Landing Qyburn tells Cersei the Faith Militant have entered the Red Keep demanding she come to the Sept. Lancel is among them. Cersei refuses, citing the Sparrow’s promise she could remain until trial. Lancel says no such promise was made. The Mountain threatens them. One Faith Militant stabs Gregor’s chest; Gregor rips the man’s head off. The others back down.

Later a royal announcement fills the throne room. Kevan bars Cersei from standing beside Tommen; she must sit with the other ladies. Tommen declares Crown and Faith are the two pillars; the Father judges all. Loras and Cersei’s trials will be on the first day of the Festival of the Mother. After prayer he abolishes trial by combat as a scheme to escape the gods’ judgment; they will be tried by seven septons as in the Faith’s early days. Cersei, who planned to use the Mountain as champion, is stunned. Qyburn whispers that his little birds have confirmed an old rumor of hers—and it is far more than rumor.

In the Riverlands Sandor finds Brotherhood men (including those who massacred the village) joking by a fire. He kills two instantly with his axe, then gut-stabs Gatins after a fight and finishes him when the man gives poor last words. Later he comes upon Beric Dondarrion and Thoros of Myr hanging Lem and two other renegades for the unauthorized attack. Sandor wants to kill them himself more painfully. Out of respect Beric lets him hang two; Thoros will not allow butchery. Sandor kicks the blocks and takes Lem’s boots while the body twitches. Afterward he shares a meal. Beric invites him to join; cold winds rise in the North and they fight things that would destroy all life. It is not too late for Sandor to help.

Brienne and Podrick reach Riverrun and find it under Lannister-Frey siege. Lannister horsemen surround them. Brienne says she has come to see Jaime and return Oathkeeper. While she speaks with Jaime, Bronn playfully chokes Podrick then offers lessons in dirty fighting.

(The siege negotiations and further Riverlands events continue, with Jaime weighing options regarding the Blackfish and Edmure.)

Episode 9: Battle of the Bastards
In Meereen the pyramid is bombarded. Daenerys wants to slaughter the masters’ army; Tyrion urges talking first, reminding her of her father’s wildfire plot and what Jaime did to stop it. They meet the masters (Razdal, Belicho, Yezzan). The masters offer safe departure if the Unsullied and Missandei are returned to slavery and the dragons killed. Daenerys says she came to offer them surrender. Drogon lands; she mounts him. Rhaegal and Viserion break free and join. They burn ships. Daario and the Dothraki arrive and rout the Sons of the Harpy at the gates. The masters’ soldiers abandon them. Tyrion demands one master die as punishment. The other two immediately scapegoat lowborn Yezzan. Grey Worm instead slits Razdal’s and Belicho’s throats. Tyrion tells Yezzan to spread word of what he saw.

Afterwards Daenerys and Tyrion meet Yara and Theon, who offer a hundred ships if she helps against Euron and supports their claim to the Iron Islands. Tyrion is wary of Theon because of Winterfell, but Theon says he has paid. Daenerys agrees to an independent Iron Islands on condition the Ironborn stop reaving, pillaging, and raping. Yara balks; Daenerys says their fathers left the world worse—together they can leave it better. Yara agrees. They seal the pact.

In the North Jon, Sansa, Tormund, Davos, Lyanna Mormont and other northern lords parley with Ramsay, Harald Karstark, and Smalljon Umber. Ramsay offers pardon if Jon hands over Sansa. Jon refuses and challenges single combat. Ramsay declines, confident in numbers, and produces Rickon (proved by tossing Shaggydog’s head). Sansa tells Ramsay he will die tomorrow and rides off.

In camp Sansa scolds Jon for not waiting for more men. He says this is all they could gather. She warns of Ramsay’s cruelty; he says he has faced worse and will protect her. She replies no one can keep anyone safe. Davos and Tormund talk of serving Stannis and Mance and wonder if they backed the wrong kings. Davos finds the remains of Shireen’s pyre and the wooden stag he carved for her; he finally understands how she died. Jon tells Melisandre not to resurrect him if he falls. She says the Lord of Light decides.

Next morning Ramsay brings Rickon out, cuts his bonds, and tells him to run to his brother. As Rickon runs, Ramsay shoots arrows. Jon races on horseback. Just as Jon nears, an arrow takes Rickon in the back. Enraged, Jon charges the Bolton lines alone. Davos sends the Stark cavalry. Bolton archers kill Jon’s horse. Ramsay orders his cavalry to crush Jon. The Stark horse smash into the Boltons, saving Jon. Davos holds his archers because the melee is too mixed. Ramsay has no such care and rains arrows on everyone, creating a corpse barrier. Bolton infantry form a three-sided shield-and-spear phalanx that compresses the Stark forces against the body pile. Smalljon leads men over the corpses to complete the encirclement. Wun Wun kills some but cannot break it. Panic spreads; Tormund urges retreat over the pile. Jon is trampled and nearly suffocates under bodies but fights free.

A horn sounds. Sansa and Littlefinger arrive with the Knights of the Vale. They smash the Bolton rear. Smalljon is distracted; Tormund bites his throat and stabs him repeatedly. Ramsay retreats into Winterfell. Jon, Wun Wun, and Tormund pursue. Wun Wun breaks the gates; wildlings pour in and slaughter the remaining garrison. Wun Wun is riddled with arrows and finally killed by Ramsay’s shot through the eye. Ramsay taunts that he will now accept single combat. Jon advances, blocking three arrows with a Mormont shield, knocks the bow away, and beats Ramsay savagely with his fists. Ramsay grins through it until the beating continues. Jon stops when he sees Sansa and has Ramsay locked up.

Stark banners replace Bolton ones. Melisandre watches the courtyard; Davos glares, clutching Shireen’s stag. Jon orders Rickon buried in the crypts beside Ned. Sansa demands to know where Ramsay is held.

That night Sansa visits the kennels. Ramsay is bound to a chair. He goads that she will never be rid of him—he is part of her now. She says his name and deeds will be forgotten. He realizes the hounds (starved for a week by his own order) are approaching, doors left open, his blood scenting them. He orders them to heel; they ignore him. As they tear him apart he screams. Sansa walks away, smiling coldly.

Episode 10: The Winds of Winter
At the Twins Jaime and Bronn arrive to celebrate the retaking of Riverrun. Walder Frey crows and compares them both as kingslayers. Jaime, insulted, mocks Walder’s lack of real battle experience and asks why the Lannisters need the Freys if they cannot even hold the Riverlands, then storms out.

Later Walder eats alone. A new servant girl serves pie. He does not recognize her and gropes her while complaining his sons Black Walder and Lothar are late. She says they are already present and indicates the pie. He opens it and sees their flesh, a fingertip with nail still attached. The girl peels off her face—Arya Stark. She tells him the last thing he will see is a Stark smiling as he dies. She cuts his throat and watches with satisfaction as the architect of the Red Wedding bleeds out.

In King’s Landing the trials of Loras and Cersei are set. Smallfolk, Margaery, Mace, and Kevan enter the Great Sept. Pycelle is lured away by a little bird. Loras is brought first. Instead of trial he confesses everything and begs to join the Faith, renouncing name and Highgarden. The Sparrows carve a seven-pointed star into his forehead. Mace tries to intervene; Margaery holds him back, then protests the mutilation. The Sparrow says he only promised Loras could leave after Cersei’s trial.

Tommen prepares to leave for the trial but the Mountain blocks him. Lancel is sent to fetch Cersei. A little bird lures Lancel into the catacombs. Another leads Pycelle to a room where Qyburn waits; children swarm and stab Pycelle to death. Margaery realizes Cersei and Tommen’s absence means a trap and tries to clear the Sept. The Faith Militant block the doors. Below, the little bird stabs Lancel in the spine. Paralyzed, Lancel sees a cache of wildfire and three candles about to ignite it. He crawls desperately. The wildfire explodes, green fire racing through the tunnels and erupting under the Sept. The High Sparrow is burned to bone; the entire Great Sept and surrounding district are obliterated in a massive blast that levels a wide area of the city.

Cersei watches from a window and smiles amid the screams. Tommen stares from his chambers in shock. Informed that Margaery is dead, he removes his crown, sets it aside, and calmly steps out the window to his death.

Cersei visits captive Septa Unella, strapped to a table. She pours wine on her to wake her, forces her to admit she enjoyed the torment for its own sake, then “confesses” her own crimes (incest, Robert’s death, the Sept massacre) because they felt good. She reminds Unella of the promise that Cersei’s face would be the last she saw, then brings in the Mountain (helmet off) to begin prolonged torture. Cersei leaves, echoing “Shame” three times as Unella screams.

After Tommen’s body is found Cersei’s triumph fades. She insists on seeing his face once more. She orders the body burned and the ashes buried where the Sept stood so he can rest with Joffrey, Myrcella, and Tywin.

Jaime and the army return to see smoke rising from the ruined Sept district. Later Cersei enters the throne room. Qyburn, now Hand, proclaims her Queen of the Seven Kingdoms and crowns her. She sits the Iron Throne. The assembled nobles glare with hatred but fear; she looks only at Jaime, whose face is stony. She has claimed the throne—or what remains of Lannister power.

In Oldtown Sam, Gilly, and little Sam arrive as white ravens are released, announcing winter. At the Citadel a maester is skeptical because records still list Jeor Mormont and Aemon, but allows Sam into the vast library (barring Gilly). Sam wanders in awe among the countless books and scrolls.

Beyond the Wall Benjen leaves Meera and Bran at a weirwood grove within sight of the Wall; the Wall’s magic bars the dead (including him). He will fight for the living as he can. Bran insists he must become the Three-Eyed Raven fully and touches the tree. He returns to the Tower of Joy vision. Ned enters and finds dying Lyanna in a blood-soaked bed. A handmaiden gives him a newborn. Lyanna whispers the baby’s identity and begs Ned to protect him—Robert would kill the child if he knew. As Ned holds his infant nephew the baby’s eyes open: dark, like Jon Snow’s. Bran understands: Jon is Lyanna’s son.

At Winterfell the northern lords gather. Lyanna Mormont shames those who held back and declares Jon the King in the North. One by one the lords (including former doubters) draw swords and acclaim him. Sansa watches with mixed feelings; Littlefinger watches Sansa. Jon is proclaimed King in the North.

In Meereen Daenerys prepares to sail for Westeros. She names Tyrion Hand of the Queen. The fleet—including the Iron Fleet ships of Yara and Theon, the ships taken from the masters, and her own—sets out. Daenerys stands at the prow of her ship with her advisors and dragons flying overhead, finally heading home to reclaim the Iron Throne.

(End of Season 6.)
