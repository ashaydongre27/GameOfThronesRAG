The movie begins with a meteorite streaking through the night sky above New York City and crashing into the ground in a wooded park area where Peter Parker and Mary Jane Watson are sharing an intimate moment under the stars. The black symbiote substance emerges from the meteorite crater and begins its journey toward the city drawn by sound and movement. This opening establishes the central disruptive force of the film without any prior assumption about its nature as an alien parasite that amplifies negative emotions in its host. Peter Parker at this stage narrates directly to the audience that his life has reached a point of genuine happiness two years after defeating Doctor Octopus. He feels he has finally balanced his responsibilities as Spider-Man with his personal desires. This narration reveals Peter's initial character state as optimistic yet subtly arrogant about his ability to maintain that balance which represents a development from the guilt-ridden and self-doubting young man seen at the end of the previous movie. His relationship with Mary Jane Watson is romantic and committed at the start. Mary Jane has developed from the frustrated aspiring actress of the second film into a woman who has achieved a lead role in a Broadway musical and who openly expresses her love for Peter while still feeling the emotional distance caused by his double life. Peter swings through the city as Spider-Man in his classic red and blue suit stopping a crime in progress with fluid acrobatics that demonstrate his established powers and his enjoyment of public admiration. This early action sequence shows how Peter has grown more confident in his heroic identity to the point where he performs for crowds which plants the seed for the ego inflation that the symbiote will later exploit.

Flint Marko emerges as a key character in the next sequence where he is shown visiting his young daughter Penny in a hospital before being apprehended by police. Flint is a desperate father whose wife has left him and whose daughter suffers from a serious illness that requires expensive treatment. This establishes Flint's primary motivation as love for his child which drives every decision he makes and makes him a sympathetic figure even as a criminal. His relation to Peter is initially nonexistent on a personal level but will become one of profound guilt and vengeance once it is revealed that Flint was the actual killer of Uncle Ben Parker in the first film rather than the carjacker Peter had previously stopped. Flint escapes from a prison transport vehicle by running into a vast particle physics testing site where sand from an experimental accelerator fuses with his body at the molecular level during a demonstration of molecular transformation technology. The process is shown in graphic detail as his body disintegrates and reforms as living sand granting him the ability to shapeshift control sand particles and survive extreme physical damage. This transformation sequence details every visual change in Flint's form from his skin turning granular to his clothes dissolving into sand and his first attempts to control his new powers which immediately mark his development from an ordinary escaped convict into the villain known as Sandman. No element of his origin is assumed beyond what is explicitly depicted including the scientists' dialogue explaining the particle accelerator's function and the accidental nature of his exposure.

Peter arrives at a theater to watch Mary Jane perform in her Broadway musical. Their interaction backstage after the show highlights their close bond as Peter praises her performance and she responds with affection while hinting at her desire for a deeper commitment. This scene develops Mary Jane's character further by showing her vulnerability about whether her acting career will sustain her and her reliance on Peter for emotional support. Their relationship is portrayed as loving but already showing minor cracks because Peter's frequent absences as Spider-Man leave her waiting alone. Peter then visits Aunt May in her new apartment. Aunt May has developed from the worried moral guide of the second film into a more reflective woman who has come to terms with her husband's death and now focuses on encouraging Peter to move forward in life. She shares stories of Uncle Ben and advises Peter on the importance of forgiveness which directly foreshadows Peter's later struggle with rage toward Flint Marko. Their surrogate mother-son relationship remains the emotional core for Peter providing him with unconditional love and ethical grounding that contrasts with the aggression the symbiote will introduce. Peter reveals his plan to propose to Mary Jane and Aunt May gives him her wedding ring which belonged to Uncle Ben strengthening the familial tie across generations.

Harry Osborn awakens from the coma he has been in since the conclusion of the previous movie. Harry is the son of the late Norman Osborn the original Green Goblin and was Peter's best friend throughout childhood and college. Their relationship has been shattered by Harry's belief that Spider-Man murdered his father which creates a deep layer of betrayal and hatred layered on top of their former brotherhood. Upon waking Harry experiences temporary amnesia that erases his knowledge of Peter's secret identity allowing a brief reconciliation where the two friends reconnect with genuine warmth. This amnesia phase develops Harry's character by showing his capacity for loyalty when not consumed by revenge but the memory returns later intensifying his vendetta. Harry equips himself with advanced Oscorp technology including a strengthened goblin glider and a new armored suit with retractable blades and explosive pumpkin bombs becoming the New Goblin. His development in this film arcs from vengeful antagonist seeking to kill Spider-Man to a temporary ally and finally to a redeemed friend who sacrifices himself which completes his growth from the grief-stricken son in the second movie.

Peter proposes to Mary Jane on the roof of a Broadway theater during a private dinner he has arranged with candles and flowers. The moment is interrupted by a sudden attack from the New Goblin who has regained his memories and now seeks revenge. The fight between Spider-Man and the New Goblin is depicted with precise choreography showing Harry using his glider for high-speed maneuvers and firing blades while Peter dodges and attempts to reason with his former friend. This battle deepens the fractured relationship between Peter and Harry by forcing Peter to defend himself without wanting to harm someone he still considers a brother. Mary Jane is kidnapped during the chaos and held hostage which heightens the stakes for Peter and strains his romantic tie to her because she witnesses firsthand the constant danger his life as Spider-Man brings. Harry is eventually defeated when Peter uses webbing to disrupt his glider and causes him to crash suffering a head injury that restores his amnesia. Doctors later inform Harry that any further stress could be fatal which adds urgency to his character arc and makes his eventual redemption more poignant as he balances revenge with self-preservation.

The symbiote meanwhile has made its way through the city attaching itself to various objects before latching onto Peter while he sleeps in his apartment. Peter wakes up feeling invigorated and notices the new suit which responds to his thoughts. This marks the beginning of his corruption as the symbiote subtly amplifies his aggression and confidence. His relationship with the suit evolves from one of curiosity and empowerment to dependency and moral compromise. Jameson has developed little from prior films remaining a bombastic editor who hates Spider-Man yet profits from his image while his staff including Betty Brant and Robbie Robertson offer Peter quiet support. Jameson's rivalry with Peter is purely transactional but becomes more personal when rival photographer Eddie Brock enters the picture.

Eddie Brock is introduced as an ambitious and unethical photographer at the Bugle who competes directly with Peter for staff photographer positions. Eddie's relationship with Peter starts as professional jealousy and escalates into outright enmity after Peter exposes one of Eddie's photos as a fake. This event leads to Eddie being fired which destroys his career and reputation. Eddie's development traces a path from smug opportunist to a man consumed by hatred who bonds with the discarded symbiote to become Venom. His connection to Peter is one of mirrored opposites both young men whose lives are upended by Spider-Man but who respond in opposite moral directions. Gwen Stacy is introduced as a classmate of Peter at Empire State University and the daughter of police Captain George Stacy. Gwen develops a brief flirtation with Peter that is amplified by the symbiote's influence on his personality making him more outgoing and cocky. This creates tension in Peter's relationship with Mary Jane who feels neglected and jealous. Captain Stacy's relation to Peter shifts from polite acquaintance to a figure of respect after he learns Peter's secret and offers understanding before dying in the climax. The relation between Peter and Flint thus becomes one of hunter and hunted with Peter driven by revenge rather than justice. In their first confrontation at the graveyard Peter attacks Sandman with newfound ferocity aided by the black suit. The fight is shown in detail with Sandman dissolving into sand to escape blows reforming behind Peter and pummeling him with massive sandy fists. Peter ultimately believes he has destroyed Sandman by sucking him into a vacuum device but this proves temporary. This sequence illustrates Peter's moral decline under the symbiote's influence as he ignores Aunt May's earlier advice about forgiveness and instead embraces rage which damages his heroic identity and strains his relationships with those who value his compassion.

The black suit's effect on Peter grows more pronounced when he arrives late to Mary Jane's important Broadway opening night. Under the suit's influence he argues with her publicly and leaves her humiliated which severely damages their romantic relationship. Mary Jane breaks up with him shortly afterward telling Peter that the man she loved seems to have changed. This breakup marks a low point in both characters' development with Mary Jane retreating into isolation and doubt about her future while Peter remains oblivious at first due to the suit's emotional suppression. Peter then attends a jazz club where he dances with Gwen Stacy in an exaggerated and arrogant manner that embarrasses her and further alienates his friends. The symbiote is shown to be feeding on his negative emotions of pride and anger turning him into someone who prioritizes self-satisfaction over empathy. This development is contrasted with his earlier narration about happiness showing how quickly the balance he claimed to have achieved has unraveled.

Realizing the suit is changing him Peter attempts to remove it in a church bell tower where the high-frequency sound weakens the symbiote. The removal process is depicted with intense struggle as the black substance writhes and screams while Peter rips it off piece by piece. The discarded symbiote falls onto Eddie Brock who is praying below for Peter's ruin. The two bond instantly creating Venom a creature that combines Eddie's knowledge of Peter's identity with the symbiote's power and hatred. Venom's first appearance shows him with a monstrous white-veined black body and a long tongue capable of speech that taunts Peter. This new villain's relation to Peter is intensely personal because Eddie knows his secret and uses it to target everyone Peter loves. Meanwhile Harry Osborn recovers his memories and allies temporarily with Venom and Sandman who has reformed after the vacuum incident. Flint's character develops further as he explains to Harry that he only wants money for his daughter and has no inherent desire to be a villain yet he agrees to the alliance out of self-interest. The trio's plot against Peter adds complexity to the web of relationships with Harry acting as the strategic leader driven by paternal loyalty Flint motivated by family desperation and Eddie by pure spite.

Peter returns to his apartment in his original suit and learns from Aunt May that Mary Jane has moved on with another man. This news combined with his rejection of the symbiote forces Peter to confront his mistakes. His development here shifts toward redemption as he apologizes to those he has hurt including Gwen and attempts to reconcile with Mary Jane. The film shows him buying flowers and waiting for her only to discover she is being held by Venom. A pivotal scene occurs when Sandman confronts Peter again in a construction site battle that demonstrates Sandman's full capabilities as he grows to enormous size by absorbing sand from the environment. The fight includes detailed choreography of Peter being buried in sand waves dodging particle blasts and using his webs to disrupt Flint's form. This encounter deepens Peter's understanding of forgiveness because Flint explicitly apologizes for killing Uncle Ben and explains his daughter's illness which softens Peter's rage and begins his final character growth back toward the responsible hero from the earlier films.

The climax unfolds in a multi-part battle on a construction site near a skyscraper. Venom and Sandman team up against Spider-Man with Venom using tendrils to restrain Peter and Sandman delivering powerful blows. Harry arrives as the New Goblin initially to fight alongside the villains but changes his mind after a vision of his father Norman urging revenge is rejected in favor of their old friendship. Harry's development reaches its peak here as he chooses loyalty to Peter over his father's legacy sacrificing himself by taking a fatal blow from Venom's tendril. This death cements the tragic end of the Osborn-Parker brotherhood that began as childhood friendship in the first film and evolved through betrayal and partial reconciliation. Peter uses the church bells again to weaken Venom separating Eddie from the symbiote. The black suit is destroyed when Peter throws a pumpkin bomb from Harry's glider into it. Eddie attempts to remerge with the symbiote but is killed in the resulting explosion. Sandman and Peter share a final moment where Peter forgives Flint who then dissipates into the wind to presumably return to his daughter. This forgiveness completes Peter's arc from vengeful and arrogant to humble and responsible showing measurable growth in his ability to let go of anger.

In the aftermath Mary Jane returns to Peter after being rescued and they reconcile on the rooftop where she acknowledges the difficulties of loving a hero but chooses to stay by his side. Their relationship is repaired with a deeper understanding than at the film's start reflecting mutual development through separation and crisis. Harry dies in Peter's arms surrounded by his friends including Mary Jane and the butler who had served the Osborn family. Harry's arc concludes with redemption and peace having resolved his hatred. The film ends with Peter attending Harry's funeral and narrating once more about the lessons of power responsibility and choice. This closing narration ties together every character's journey emphasizing that Peter's growth required facing his flaws amplified by the symbiote and that true heroism lies in balancing all relationships without letting any one dominate. J. Aunt May offers Peter final words of pride reinforcing their enduring maternal bond that has guided his development across all three films. Spider-Man 3 released in 2007 continues the story of Peter Parker directly from the end of the previous film.
