🐺 SEASON 1 – "Winter Is Coming" (2011)
Season 1 introduces the sprawling world of Westeros, a continent ruled from the Iron Throne in King's Landing. The story begins in the North, where Lord Eddard "Ned" Stark rules Winterfell alongside his wife Catelyn and their children: Robb, Sansa, Arya, Bran, Rickon, and Ned's bastard son Jon Snow. The season opens with a chilling prologue beyond the Wall, where members of the Night's Watch encounter the White Walkers — ancient, supernatural beings thought to be myth.

King Robert Baratheon, Ned's oldest friend and the man who overthrew the Mad King Aerys Targaryen years earlier, travels north to Winterfell after the sudden death of his Hand, Jon Arryn. Robert asks Ned to serve as the new Hand of the King. Ned reluctantly accepts, sensing that something is deeply wrong. His suspicion grows when Catelyn receives a secret letter from her sister Lysa Arryn, claiming Jon Arryn was murdered by the Lannisters — Queen Cersei's powerful family.

Before Ned departs, tragedy strikes. His young son Bran climbs a tower and witnesses Queen Cersei in an incestuous embrace with her twin brother, Ser Jaime Lannister. To protect their secret, Jaime pushes Bran from the window, leaving him crippled and comatose. Bran eventually wakes with no memory of the fall but discovers he can no longer walk.

Meanwhile, across the Narrow Sea in Essos, the exiled Targaryen siblings Viserys and Daenerys scheme to reclaim the Iron Throne. Viserys sells his sister into marriage to Khal Drogo, a fearsome Dothraki warlord, in exchange for an army. Daenerys, initially terrified, grows into her role as Khaleesi, falling in love with Drogo and becoming pregnant with a son prophesied to be "The Stallion Who Mounts the World." She is also given three petrified dragon eggs as a wedding gift.

Jon Snow, feeling out of place as a bastard, travels to the Wall to join the Night's Watch — a lifelong brotherhood sworn to defend the realm against threats from beyond. There he meets Samwell Tarly, a cowardly but kind-hearted noble who becomes his closest friend. Jon struggles with his vows but is inspired by his uncle Benjen Stark, a ranger who soon disappears beyond the Wall.

In King's Landing, Ned investigates Jon Arryn's death with the help of the cunning Petyr "Littlefinger" Baelish and the eunuch spymaster Varys. He discovers that all of Robert's "children" — Joffrey, Myrcella, and Tommen — are actually the products of incest between Cersei and Jaime. This means Joffrey has no legitimate claim to the throne.

Catelyn, meanwhile, encounters Tyrion Lannister — Cersei's dwarf brother — and arrests him for allegedly hiring an assassin to kill Bran. She takes him to the Eyrie, where her unstable sister Lysa rules. Tyrion demands a trial by combat, and the sellsword Bronn wins his freedom.

King Robert dies in a suspicious hunting "accident" arranged by Cersei. Before dying, he names Ned as Protector of the Realm. Ned attempts to expose Joffrey's illegitimacy but is betrayed by Littlefinger and arrested for treason. In one of the most shocking moments in television history, King Joffrey orders Ned beheaded in front of his daughter Sansa, who is being held as a hostage-betrothed. Arya, disguised as a boy, escapes the city with the help of the Night's Watch recruiter Yoren.

Robb Stark raises the northern armies to march south, winning his first battle and capturing Jaime Lannister. The northern lords proclaim him "King in the North." Catelyn negotiates with the volatile Lord Walder Frey, promising Robb will marry one of Frey's daughters in exchange for crossing his bridge.

Across the sea, Khal Drogo is wounded and falls into a fevered stupor. Daenerys turns to a witch, Mirri Maz Duur, for blood magic, but the ritual costs her unborn child and leaves Drogo in a vegetative state. Daenerys smothers her husband mercifully and builds a funeral pyre, placing her dragon eggs on the flames and walking into the fire herself. In the season's stunning finale, she emerges unburnt at dawn with three newly hatched baby dragons — the first dragons seen in the world in over a century.

⚔️ SEASON 2 – "The War of the Five Kings" (2012)
Season 2 plunges Westeros into full-scale civil war. With Robert dead and Joffrey on the throne, multiple claimants rise up. Robert's younger brothers, Stannis and Renly Baratheon, both declare themselves king. Robb Stark continues his northern rebellion, and Balon Greyjoy of the Iron Islands declares independence, crowning himself king as well.

Stannis, the rightful heir by law, is stern and joyless but honorable. He falls under the influence of Melisandre, a red priestess of the fire god R'hllor, who convinces him he is Azor Ahai, a prophesied hero. She births a shadow assassin from her womb that murders Renly, causing most of Renly's forces — and his secret lover, Ser Loras Tyrell — to scatter. Renly's widow, Margaery Tyrell, and her clever grandmother Olenna begin their own political maneuvering.

Tyrion Lannister arrives in King's Landing as acting Hand of the King, sent by his father Tywin. Tyrion proves to be a brilliant political operator, outmaneuvering Cersei, curbing Joffrey's worst cruelties, and preparing the city's defenses. He identifies the leaks in the small council by feeding different lies to each member.

Arya Stark, disguised as a boy named "Arry," is captured when the Lannisters attack Yoren's caravan. She ends up as a cupbearer at Harrenhal, the massive ruined castle now serving as Lord Tywin Lannister's headquarters. She saves the life of the mysterious assassin Jaqen H'ghar, who repays her by killing three people of her choosing. She uses her three names to help fellow prisoners escape.

Bran rules Winterfell in his brother Robb's absence, guided by Maester Luwin and the wildling servant Osha. Bran begins having strange prophetic dreams as a wolf — the first hints of his "warg" abilities. Theon Greyjoy, Ned Stark's former ward, betrays the Starks by returning to his father Balon and captures Winterfell. When Bran and Rickon escape, Theon murders two farm boys and burns their bodies, passing them off as the young Stark princes to save face.

Jon Snow ranges beyond the Wall with the legendary Qhorin Halfhand. He captures a wildling woman, Ygritte, and becomes fascinated by her. Eventually, Qhorin orders Jon to pretend to defect to the wildlings — the "Free Folk" — to spy on them. Jon kills Qhorin in a staged fight to prove his loyalty and is taken to meet Mance Rayder, the King-Beyond-the-Wall.

Sam and his brothers of the Night's Watch discover ancient dragonglass weapons buried near the Fist of the First Men, foreshadowing the coming war against the White Walkers.

Daenerys arrives in the city of Qarth with her fledgling dragons, seeking ships and allies. She is betrayed by the warlocks of the House of the Undying, who steal her dragons. She rescues them and locks the treacherous merchant Xaro Xhoan Daxos in his own empty vault.

The season culminates in the epic Battle of the Blackwater. Stannis's fleet sails to King's Landing to seize the throne, but Tyrion unleashes wildfire — a green explosive substance — that incinerates most of Stannis's ships. Just as Stannis's remaining troops breach the walls, Tywin Lannister arrives with the Tyrell army as reinforcements, saving the city. Tyrion is wounded and left horribly scarred, while Tywin claims all the credit and installs himself as the new Hand of the King. Joffrey, on his family's political maneuvering, sets aside Sansa and becomes betrothed to Margaery Tyrell.

The season ends with a chilling image: Sam, hiding behind a rock beyond the Wall, watches in horror as a massive army of wights and White Walkers marches south.

🗡️ SEASON 3 – "Chaos Is a Ladder" (2013)
Season 3 is defined by shifting alliances, deepening tragedy, and one of the most infamous betrayals in fiction. Robb Stark's rebellion begins to falter. Though he has never lost a battle, he loses the political war. Angry at his mother Catelyn for freeing Jaime Lannister (in a desperate attempt to trade him for her daughters), Robb also breaks his oath to marry a Frey daughter after falling in love with and marrying Talisa, a foreign healer.

Jaime Lannister travels north under the guard of Brienne of Tarth, a towering, honorable female knight sworn to Catelyn Stark. Their journey is antagonistic at first but grows into deep mutual respect. They are captured by Roose Bolton's men, and Jaime has his sword hand — his identity as a warrior — brutally chopped off by the sadistic Locke. Broken and humbled, Jaime slowly transforms, saving Brienne from a bear pit and returning her to King's Landing with him.

In King's Landing, Margaery Tyrell skillfully manipulates the cruel Joffrey by pretending to enjoy his violent interests. Sansa becomes a pawn in the Lannisters' schemes and is forcibly married to Tyrion, who treats her with unexpected kindness but never consummates the marriage. Cersei is horrified to learn she is to be married off to Loras Tyrell. Tywin manipulates everyone like pieces on a board.

Arya, having escaped Harrenhal, is captured by the Brotherhood Without Banners — a group of outlaws led by Beric Dondarrion, who has been resurrected six times by the red priest Thoros of Myr. The Brotherhood sells the Hound (Sandor Clegane) to the highest bidder, but he escapes with Arya as his prisoner, planning to ransom her to her family.

Theon Greyjoy is captured, tortured, and psychologically broken by a mysterious sadist who is later revealed to be Ramsay Snow, Roose Bolton's bastard son. Ramsay flays parts of Theon's body, castrates him, and renames him "Reek" — reducing him to a broken, terrified shell of himself.

Bran, Rickon, Osha, and the wildling brothers Hodor journey north with the Reeds — Jojen and Meera — seeking the mysterious three-eyed raven. Jojen recognizes Bran as a powerful greenseer. They separate: Rickon goes with Osha for safety, while Bran continues north.

Jon Snow lives among the wildlings, falling deeply in love with Ygritte. He climbs the 700-foot Wall with them but ultimately reveals his true loyalties, escapes, and returns wounded to Castle Black. Ygritte, heartbroken, shoots him full of arrows before he escapes. At the Fist of the First Men, the Night's Watch is decimated by the White Walkers. Sam kills a White Walker with a dragonglass dagger — the first person in centuries to do so — and escapes with a wildling woman named Gilly and her infant son.

Stannis broods on Dragonstone while Melisandre becomes obsessed with king's blood. She captures Gendry, Robert Baratheon's bastard son, and uses his blood in rituals. Davos Seaworth, Stannis's loyal Hand and former smuggler, secretly frees Gendry to save him from being sacrificed.

Daenerys arrives in Slaver's Bay and begins her conquest. In Astapor, she buys an army of 8,000 Unsullied — elite eunuch slave-soldiers — by pretending to trade one of her dragons. She then commands her dragon to burn the slave masters and orders the Unsullied to kill every slaver in the city, freeing all slaves. She then marches on Yunkai and frees that city too, being hailed by the freed slaves as "Mhysa" — Mother.

The season's centerpiece is the Red Wedding. Robb, Catelyn, and their army arrive at the Twins for Edmure Tully's wedding to a Frey daughter — a peace offering after Robb broke his own oath. During the feast, Walder Frey and Roose Bolton spring their trap. Talisa is stabbed in her pregnant belly, Robb is shot with crossbows, Catelyn's throat is slit, and their army is massacred. The North's rebellion dies in a single night of horror. Arya arrives just in time to witness the aftermath and is dragged away by the Hound.

The season ends with the northern kingdom shattered, the Lannisters ascendant, and Daenerys rising in the east.

👑 SEASON 4 – "The Lion and the Rose" (2014)
Season 4 opens in the aftermath of the Red Wedding, with the Lannisters seemingly triumphant. But their victory quickly unravels. At the wedding of King Joffrey to Margaery Tyrell — the "Purple Wedding" — Joffrey is poisoned mid-toast, dying horribly in his mother's arms. Cersei accuses Tyrion of murder, and he is arrested.

The real murderers are Olenna Tyrell and Petyr Baelish, who orchestrated the poisoning to place the gentler Tommen on the throne while protecting Margaery. Sansa is smuggled out of the city by Littlefinger's agent, Ser Dontos, who is then killed to silence him.

Tyrion's trial is a farce. Every witness lies, including his former lover Shae, whose betrayal devastates him. In a legendary courtroom outburst, Tyrion demands trial by combat. The dashing Prince Oberyn Martell of Dorne — who has come to King's Landing seeking vengeance for his sister Elia, raped and murdered by the Mountain during Robert's Rebellion — volunteers to fight for Tyrion against Ser Gregor "the Mountain" Clegane. Oberyn nearly wins, dancing around the giant with a spear, but in his obsession with a confession, he lets his guard down. The Mountain crushes his skull in a horrifying moment. Tyrion is sentenced to death.

Jaime frees Tyrion from his cell the night before his execution. Tyrion detours to his father's chambers, where he finds Shae in Tywin's bed. He strangles her, then confronts Tywin on the privy and shoots him dead with a crossbow. Tyrion escapes King's Landing with Varys, sailing east.

Sansa is taken to the Eyrie by Littlefinger, who marries her aunt Lysa. When Lysa attempts to kill Sansa out of jealousy, Littlefinger pushes Lysa through the Moon Door to her death, becoming Sansa's sole "protector."

Arya and the Hound travel across the Riverlands, forming a strange, darkly comic bond. They encounter Brienne, who is searching for the Stark girls per her oath to Catelyn. Brienne and the Hound duel savagely; Brienne wins, but Arya slips away and leaves the mortally wounded Hound to die. She uses a coin given to her by Jaqen H'ghar to book passage to Braavos.

At the Wall, Jon Snow returns and warns of the wildling army approaching. Mance Rayder's forces attack Castle Black in the epic Battle of Castle Black. The Night's Watch, hopelessly outnumbered, holds off tens of thousands of wildlings in a brutal night of combat. Ygritte is killed by a young boy, dying in Jon's arms. Jon marches out alone to negotiate — or assassinate — Mance, but Stannis arrives with his army just in time, having been convinced by Davos that saving the realm is the true path to becoming king.

Beyond the Wall, Bran and his companions reach the weirwood tree of the Three-Eyed Raven, but are attacked by wights. Jojen Reed is killed. Bran, warging into Hodor, escapes into the cave with Meera. The Three-Eyed Raven — an ancient man fused with the roots of a weirwood — greets Bran and promises to teach him to fly.

Daenerys conquers Meereen, the largest city in Slaver's Bay, and decides to stay and rule rather than sail to Westeros immediately. Her dragons grow larger and more uncontrollable. When one, Drogon, burns a peasant child, Daenerys is forced to chain up her other two dragons, Rhaegal and Viserion, in the catacombs — a heartbreaking betrayal of her "children."

Theon, now fully broken as Reek, is used by Ramsay to retake Moat Cailin for the Boltons. Roose Bolton is granted Wardenship of the North by the crown, and Ramsay is legitimized as Ramsay Bolton.

The season ends with Arya sailing to Braavos, Tyrion en route to a new life, Cersei declaring her incestuous relationship with Jaime openly to Tywin (before his death), and Bran arriving at his mystical destination. The old order is collapsing.

🔥 SEASON 5 – "The Wars to Come" (2015)
Season 5 is transitional, setting up massive events while exploring themes of faith, fanaticism, and failed leadership. Tyrion arrives in Pentos and is convinced by Varys to travel east to meet Daenerys, believing she is the leader Westeros needs. Along the way, they are captured by Jorah Mormont — Daenerys's exiled advisor who was banished for spying — and Jorah decides to bring Tyrion to Daenerys as a gift to earn back her favor.

In Meereen, Daenerys struggles to rule a city where former slave masters have gone underground as "the Sons of the Harpy," conducting a terrorist insurgency. She executes a former slave for killing a captive Son of the Harpy without trial, causing a riot among her own supporters. She reopens the fighting pits to appease the local culture, and marries a Meereenese noble, Hizdahr zo Loraq, for political stability.

At the reopening of the pits, the Sons of the Harpy launch a massive coordinated attack, killing Hizdahr and hundreds of others. Just as Daenerys is about to be killed, her dragon Drogon arrives, unseen for weeks. Daenerys climbs onto his back and flies away — the first Targaryen to ride a dragon in over a century. She lands in a Dothraki grassland and is soon surrounded by a khalasar.

In King's Landing, Cersei's paranoia grows. She empowers a religious order called the Sparrows and their zealot leader, the High Sparrow, to arrest her enemies. This backfires spectacularly when the High Sparrow arrests Loras Tyrell for homosexuality, then Margaery for lying about it, and finally Cersei herself for her adultery with her cousin Lancel. Cersei is forced to undergo a "Walk of Atonement" — shaved bald and paraded naked through the streets of King's Landing while crowds jeer and throw filth at her. She arrives at the Red Keep broken but ready for vengeance, greeted by a monstrous reanimated Ser Gregor Clegane, revived by the mad maester Qyburn.

Jaime and Bronn travel to Dorne to rescue Jaime's daughter/niece Myrcella from the vengeful Sand Snakes — Oberyn's illegitimate daughters. They succeed, but as they sail home, Ellaria Sand — Oberyn's lover — kisses Myrcella with poisoned lips. Myrcella dies in Jaime's arms, revealing she knew he was her father.

Sansa returns to the North in one of the show's most controversial storylines. Littlefinger marries her off to Ramsay Bolton to gain a foothold in the North. Ramsay brutalizes Sansa on their wedding night, forcing Theon/Reek to watch. Sansa endures repeated abuse until Theon finally finds his courage. In the season finale, they leap from Winterfell's walls together to escape.

Arya arrives at the House of Black and White in Braavos, training under Jaqen H'ghar and the Waif to become a Faceless Man — an assassin who must give up her identity. She struggles to abandon "Arya Stark." When she takes an unsanctioned kill against Meryn Trant (a name on her list), she is punished with blindness.

At the Wall, Jon Snow is elected Lord Commander of the Night's Watch. Stannis executes Mance Rayder for refusing to bend the knee, but Jon shoots Mance with an arrow to spare him a slow death by fire. Jon rides north to Hardhome to evacuate the wildlings, believing they will be needed as allies against the White Walkers. At Hardhome, the Night King and his army attack, slaughtering thousands. Jon kills a White Walker with his Valyrian steel sword, Longclaw, learning that Valyrian steel can also destroy them. He escapes by ship as the Night King raises all the dead — thousands of new wights — with a simple gesture, in one of the most terrifying scenes in the series.

Stannis marches on Winterfell but is undone by winter storms and desertion. In desperation, Melisandre convinces him to sacrifice his own daughter Shireen — his only child — by burning her at the stake. His wife hangs herself in horror, his troops desert, and his army is annihilated by the Boltons. Brienne finds a wounded Stannis in the woods and executes him for the murder of Renly years earlier.

The season ends with what appeared at the time to be Jon Snow's death. His fellow brothers of the Night's Watch, angry at his alliance with the wildlings, stab him repeatedly in a Caesar-like betrayal, leaving him bleeding out in the snow. "For the Watch."

🐉 SEASON 6 – "The Winds of Winter" (2016)
Season 6 is a season of resurrection, revelation, and reclamation — the moment the show shifts from tragedy toward triumph. Melisandre, her faith shattered by Stannis's failure, is convinced by Davos to attempt a resurrection ritual on Jon Snow's body. To everyone's shock, it works. Jon returns from death, forever changed. He hangs his mutineers, declares his Watch ended (since he technically died), and leaves Castle Black.

Sansa reunites with Jon at Castle Black — their first Stark reunion in years. Together they raise an army to retake Winterfell from Ramsay Bolton. They gather support from smaller houses, aided by Ser Davos and Tormund Giantsbane. The tiny young Lady Lyanna Mormont steals the show with her fierce loyalty to the Starks.

The Battle of the Bastards is the season's centerpiece — a brutal, muddy, terrifying battle depicting the chaos of medieval warfare. Rickon Stark, captured by Ramsay, is killed running toward Jon. Jon's forces are nearly overwhelmed but are saved at the last moment by Sansa and Littlefinger arriving with the Knights of the Vale. Winterfell is reclaimed. Sansa feeds Ramsay to his own starving hounds. Jon is proclaimed King in the North by the northern lords.

Bran continues his training with the Three-Eyed Raven, using visions to travel through time. He witnesses his father's youth and, most importantly, the Tower of Joy — where young Ned Stark found his sister Lyanna dying after childbirth. Her child, Ned's "bastard" nephew, is Jon Snow — meaning Jon is actually the son of Lyanna Stark and Rhaegar Targaryen, heir to the Iron Throne. Bran also learns that the White Walkers were created by the Children of the Forest as a weapon against humans. When the Night King discovers Bran spying on him, he attacks the weirwood cave. The Three-Eyed Raven is killed, Hodor sacrifices himself holding the door ("Hold the door" — Hodor), and Bran escapes, now the new Three-Eyed Raven.

Arya recovers her sight and is finally accepted by the Faceless Men, but she rejects her training. When ordered to assassinate an actress, she instead saves her, and is hunted by the Waif. After a lengthy chase, Arya kills the Waif and declares to Jaqen, "A girl is Arya Stark of Winterfell, and I'm going home."

In King's Landing, Cersei faces trial by the Faith. On the day of her trial at the Sept of Baelor, she doesn't show up. Instead, she detonates massive stores of wildfire beneath the Sept, incinerating the High Sparrow, Margaery, Loras, Mace Tyrell, and hundreds of others. Her son Tommen, watching from the Red Keep and horrified by his wife's death, jumps out a window to his death. Cersei crowns herself Queen of the Seven Kingdoms with Jaime — returning from war — watching in horror.

Daenerys, captured by the Dothraki, is brought to Vaes Dothrak. When the khals refuse to follow a woman, she burns them all alive by torching the temple, emerging unburnt once again. She returns to Meereen with a united Dothraki horde. She rides Drogon into battle against the slavers' fleet besieging her city, unleashing all three dragons for the first time. She wins decisively.

Yara and Theon Greyjoy arrive in Meereen, having fled their murderous uncle Euron, who killed their father Balon and seized the Iron Islands. They offer Daenerys the Iron Fleet in exchange for supporting Yara's claim. Tyrion, now Daenerys's Hand, forges alliances with Dorne (the Sand Snakes have murdered Prince Doran and taken over) and the Tyrells (Olenna, now the last of her house, seeks vengeance against Cersei).

Sam and Gilly travel to Oldtown so Sam can train as a maester, stopping at Sam's cruel family home along the way. Sam steals his family's Valyrian steel sword, Heartsbane.

The season ends with a triumphant sequence: Daenerys finally sails for Westeros with the largest army the world has ever seen — Unsullied, Dothraki, three grown dragons, and the fleets of the Ironborn, Dornish, and Tyrell. The great war is about to begin.

❄️ SEASON 7 – "Dragonstone" (2017)
Season 7 is shorter (7 episodes) and faster-paced, bringing the show's disparate storylines rushing together. Daenerys arrives at Dragonstone, her ancestral home, empty and abandoned. She begins planning her conquest with her advisors Tyrion, Varys, and Missandei, and her general Grey Worm.

Cersei rules a weakened kingdom with dwindling allies. She partners with Euron Greyjoy, who offers her his massive fleet and proposes marriage. Euron ambushes Yara's fleet, capturing Yara and Ellaria Sand, and killing two of the Sand Snakes. Theon, in cowardice, jumps overboard, abandoning his sister. Cersei tortures Ellaria by chaining her opposite her poisoned daughter's corpse, promising to make her watch her rot.

Daenerys's early campaign goes poorly due to Tyrion's overly cautious strategy of using only her Westerosi allies. Euron destroys the Ironborn fleet. Jaime and the Lannister forces sack Highgarden, killing Olenna Tyrell — who gets the last word by revealing on her deathbed that SHE poisoned Joffrey. Only Daenerys's Unsullied succeed, taking Casterly Rock — only to find it strategically empty, as Jaime moved the Lannister army elsewhere.

Frustrated, Daenerys deploys her Dothraki and Drogon in the "Loot Train Attack," annihilating the Lannister army returning from Highgarden. Jaime narrowly survives after a suicidal charge at Daenerys, saved by Bronn. The scene marks the first full use of dragon warfare — devastating and morally complex.

Meanwhile, Jon Snow, King in the North, receives Sam's discovery that Dragonstone sits on a mountain of dragonglass. Jon travels to meet Daenerys, leaving Sansa in charge of Winterfell. Their first meeting is tense — she demands he bend the knee; he refuses, warning her the true war is against the dead. Slowly, they develop mutual respect and attraction. Daenerys allows Jon to mine the dragonglass.

To convince Cersei of the threat, Jon leads a mission beyond the Wall to capture a wight and bring it south. He's joined by the Hound, Tormund, Beric Dondarrion, Thoros of Myr, Jorah Mormont, and Gendry (who was rowing since Season 3). They are trapped on a frozen lake, surrounded by the army of the dead. Gendry runs back for help, and Daenerys flies north with all three dragons. She rescues them — but the Night King throws an ice spear and kills the dragon Viserion. The Night King raises the dead dragon as an undead wight, gaining an ice dragon.

At the summit in the Dragonpit at King's Landing, all the major players gather. Jon shows Cersei the wight. Cersei pretends to agree to a truce, but secretly plans to let her enemies destroy each other. Jaime, horrified by her deception, abandons her and rides north to fight the dead.

At Winterfell, Sansa and Arya are reunited, but Littlefinger works to drive them apart, hoping to eliminate Arya. Instead, the Stark sisters, aided by Bran's omniscience, expose Littlefinger's crimes: betraying Ned Stark, murdering Lysa Arryn, orchestrating the war of the Five Kings. Sansa sentences him to death and Arya slits his throat.

The season's final revelation is delivered by Bran and Sam together: Jon Snow is not a bastard. His real name is Aegon Targaryen, the legitimate son of Rhaegar and Lyanna, who were secretly married. Jon is the true heir to the Iron Throne. This bombshell is intercut with Jon and Daenerys — aunt and nephew — consummating their relationship aboard a ship, unaware of their true relation.

The season ends with the Night King mounting the undead Viserion and breathing blue flame at Eastwatch-by-the-Sea, destroying the Wall and leading his massive army into the Seven Kingdoms.

🏰 SEASON 8 – "The Long Night and the Last War" (2019)
The final, controversial season compresses enormous events into just six episodes. The armies of Daenerys and Jon gather at Winterfell to face the Night King. Jaime arrives alone to fight, having abandoned Cersei. Theon returns to protect Bran. Jon learns his true parentage from Sam and Bran — a revelation that shakes his identity and complicates his relationship with Daenerys, who sees him as a rival claimant.

The Battle of Winterfell — "The Long Night" — is the largest battle ever filmed for television. In a desperate, dark, chaotic night, the forces of the living are overwhelmed by hordes of undead. Dozens of major and minor characters die: Dolorous Edd, Lyanna Mormont (killing an undead giant), Beric Dondarrion, Theon Greyjoy (defending Bran), Jorah Mormont (defending Daenerys), and Melisandre (who lights the Dothraki weapons ablaze, then removes her enchanted necklace and dies of old age at dawn). The dragons battle in the sky. Bran uses himself as bait in the godswood.

Just as the Night King approaches Bran to kill him, Arya — trained by the Faceless Men and armed with a Valyrian steel dagger — leaps from the shadows and drives the blade into his heart, shattering him and destroying all the wights in one instant. The war for the dawn is won.

But the true final war has just begun. Daenerys, grieving Jorge and losing her dragon Rhaegal to Euron's fleet (killed by giant crossbows called scorpions), grows increasingly isolated. Missandei, her closest friend, is captured and executed by Cersei outside King's Landing. Varys, learning Jon's true identity, plots to replace Daenerys and is executed by dragonfire.

At the Battle of King's Landing, Daenerys's forces easily breach the city walls. The Lannister soldiers surrender, bells ringing. But something breaks in Daenerys. Consumed by grief, isolation, and the belief that she must rule through fear if not love, she unleashes Drogon on the surrendered city. In one of the most shocking sequences in the series, she burns King's Landing to the ground — soldiers, women, children, everyone. The Hound reaches Cersei just as she and Jaime attempt to flee. He convinces his brother Arya to abandon her revenge and leave the city. He then fights his brother, the reanimated Mountain, in "Cleganebowl," ultimately tackling him into the flames below, killing them both. Jaime and Cersei find each other in the crypts beneath the Red Keep, embracing one final time as the ceiling collapses on them.

In the aftermath, Tyrion resigns as Hand and is arrested for treason after freeing his brother. Jon, horrified by Daenerys's atrocities and her plans to "liberate" the entire world through fire and blood, is convinced by Tyrion that she cannot be allowed to continue. In a heartbreaking scene, Jon embraces Daenerys before the melted Iron Throne and stabs her through the heart. Drogon, sensing his mother's death, melts the Iron Throne itself with dragonfire — a symbolic destruction of the object that caused so much suffering — and flies away with her body.

The lords of Westeros gather to decide the future. Tyrion proposes that Bran the Broken, with his vast knowledge of the past, be made king, and that future rulers be chosen by council rather than birth. All agree except Sansa, who declares the North will remain independent. Bran is crowned "Bran the Broken, First of His Name," and appoints Tyrion as his Hand as penance.

The series ends with each Stark going their own way: Sansa is crowned Queen in the North. Jon is exiled to the Night's Watch again as a compromise between the North and the Unsullied, but he rides north beyond the Wall with Tormund and the Free Folk — where he truly belongs. Arya sails west of Westeros to explore the uncharted world. Bran rules the Six Kingdoms with a small council that includes Tyrion, Bronn (Master of Coin), Sam (Grand Maester), Davos, and Brienne — who completes Jaime's entry in the Book of Brothers, honoring him despite his flaws.

The story of the Iron Throne ends not with a triumphant conqueror, but with the wheel Daenerys promised to break — broken, at unimaginable cost, and replaced with something new but uncertain.
